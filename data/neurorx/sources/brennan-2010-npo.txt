Section: Management of patients who are nil by mouth (NPO)
Abrupt cessation of antiparkinsonian medication must be avoided because of the risk of acute akinesia, aspiration, neuroleptic malignant-like syndrome (parkinsonism-hyperpyrexia syndrome), and severe disability.

Rotigotine transdermal patch conversion:
When oral medication cannot be administered and enteral tube access is unavailable, transdermal rotigotine patch provides continuous dopaminergic stimulation.
To convert oral antiparkinsonian medication to a rotigotine patch:
Calculate the total levodopa equivalent daily dose (LEDD in mg). Divide total daily LEDD by 30 to determine the 24-hour rotigotine patch dose in mg.
Patches are available in strengths of 2 mg/24h, 4 mg/24h, 6 mg/24h, and 8 mg/24h. The maximum standard recommended daily patch dose is 16 mg/24h. Do not cut rotigotine patches.

Enteral tube administration:
If a nasogastric (NG) tube or gastrostomy is in place, dispersible levodopa/benserazide or crushed standard immediate-release levodopa tablets may be given through the tube, maintaining equivalent total daily levodopa dose divided across the day.
