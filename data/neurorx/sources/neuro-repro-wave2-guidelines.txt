Pregnancy, Teratology, and Peripartum Pharmacology in Headache, Neuroimmunology, and Neuromuscular Disorders: Consensus Guidelines (AHS, ECTRIMS/EAN, MGFA, ACR) and Pregnancy Exposure Registries

=== SECTION 1: PRECONCEPTION FOLIC ACID & CONTRACEPTION / WASHOUT GUIDANCE ===
Mycophenolate REMS & Folic Acid: Mycophenolate mofetil is a major human teratogen requiring two negative pregnancy tests prior to initiation and high-dose folic acid supplementation (4 to 5 mg/day) if inadvertent first-trimester exposure occurs, though folate does not eliminate mycophenolate embryopathy.
Mycophenolate Contraception & Washout: Per REMS mandates, females of reproductive potential must use one highly effective contraceptive method (e.g. IUD or implant) or two complementary methods during therapy and for 6 weeks after discontinuation; male partners should avoid fathering children during and for 90 days after cessation.
Rituximab Contraception & Washout: FDA labeling advises effective contraception during rituximab therapy and for 12 months after the last infusion, whereas ECTRIMS/EAN consensus guidelines allow planned conception 1 to 3 months post-infusion in active MS or NMOSD when maternal relapse risk outweighs theoretical fetal depletion.
Azathioprine Folic Acid & Contraception: Azathioprine does not reduce hormonal contraceptive efficacy, and standard preconception folic acid supplementation (0.4 to 1 mg/day) is recommended for women of childbearing potential with autoimmune neuromuscular or demyelinating disorders.
Eculizumab Contraception & Washout: Eculizumab does not interact with hormonal contraceptives; effective contraception is recommended during treatment and for 3 months after the last dose when clinically feasible, though therapy should be continued through pregnancy in life-threatening NMOSD or gMG when indicated.
Ravulizumab Contraception & Washout: Ravulizumab has an extended terminal half-life (~50 days) and does not alter hormonal contraceptive PK; effective contraception is advised during therapy and for 8 months after the final infusion unless continued treatment is required for severe NMOSD or generalized myasthenia gravis.
Efgartigimod Contraception & Washout: Efgartigimod is a neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn) blocker without CYP-mediated contraceptive interactions; transition or washout (at least 4 to 6 weeks prior to conception) is advised unless required for refractory generalized myasthenia gravis.
Erenumab Contraception & Washout: Erenumab is an IgG2 anti-CGRP receptor monoclonal antibody (~28-day half-life) with no interaction with combined oral contraceptives; AHS headache guidelines recommend discontinuing erenumab at least 5 to 6 months prior to planned conception.
Rimegepant Contraception & Washout: Rimegepant does not reduce ethinylestradiol or norelgestromin exposure from oral contraceptives; due to its ~11-hour elimination half-life, a washout period of 5 to 7 days prior to planned conception is reasonable for migraine prophylaxis.
NSAIDs Periconceptional Contraception & Ovulation (Ibuprofen, Celecoxib): Nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs do not interact with hormonal contraceptives, though chronic periconceptional COX inhibition can reversibly delay ovulation and impair implantation; discontinue in patients experiencing difficulty conceiving.
Analgesics & Preventive Agents Without Contraceptive Interactions (Acetaminophen, Sumatriptan, Amitriptyline, Duloxetine, Tramadol, Metoprolol): These medications do not induce CYP3A4 or UGT metabolism of hormonal contraceptives and require no extra contraceptive backup beyond standard family planning.
Myasthenia & Spasticity Agents Contraception Note (Pyridostigmine, Prednisolone, Methylprednisolone, Dexamethasone, IVIG, Baclofen, Tizanidine, Dantrolene): Systemic corticosteroids, pyridostigmine, IVIG, and oral muscle relaxants do not impair hormonal contraceptive efficacy; note that tizanidine clearance is reduced by combined oral contraceptives containing ethinylestradiol (CYP1A2 inhibition), increasing maternal hypotension and sedation risk.

=== SECTION 2: PREGNANCY SAFETY TIER, PHARMACOKINETICS & MALFORMATION REGISTRIES ===
Acetaminophen Preferred First-Line & Registry: Acetaminophen is the preferred first-line acute analgesic and antipyretic throughout all trimesters of pregnancy at the lowest effective dose for the shortest duration; Danish and Norwegian National Birth Cohort data reported major congenital malformations in 3.1% (95% CI: 2.9%–3.3%) of 26,424 first-trimester exposures.
Acetaminophen Pregnancy PK: Pregnancy slightly increases acetaminophen glucuronidation clearance, though standard therapeutic dosing (maximum 3,000–4,000 mg/day) applies without routine serum monitoring.
Sumatriptan Registry & PK: The Sumatriptan, Naratriptan, and Treximet Pregnancy Registry recorded major congenital malformations in 4.2% (95% CI: 2.6%–6.5%) of 528 first-trimester exposures without a specific teratogenic pattern; oral absorption may be delayed by migraine-associated gastroparesis, and standard acute dosing requires no TDM.
Erenumab Pregnancy Safety & PK: Erenumab is an IgG2 monoclonal antibody that crosses the placenta increasingly via FcRn during the second and third trimesters; human pregnancy registry data remain limited and theoretical CGRP blockade raises concern for gestational hypertension or impaired uteroplacental vasodilation.
Rimegepant Pregnancy Safety & PK: Human pregnancy outcome data for rimegepant are insufficient to exclude fetal risk; because CGRP signaling regulates uteroplacental blood flow, discontinue during pregnancy unless maternal benefit clearly outweighs potential risk.
Amitriptyline Registry & PK: The Swedish Medical Birth Register and OTIS Tricyclic Cohort reported major congenital malformations in 2.6% (95% CI: 1.9%–3.5%) of 1,662 first-trimester exposures; CYP2D6 induction during pregnancy may modestly lower plasma levels, and third-trimester exposure requires monitoring neonates for transient anticholinergic or withdrawal symptoms.
Amitriptyline Postpartum: If amitriptyline dosage was increased in late pregnancy, taper back toward pre-pregnancy maintenance levels over 1 to 2 weeks postpartum as CYP2D6 activity normalizes to minimize maternal sedation.
Duloxetine Registry & PK: Lilly surveillance and Nordic National Pregnancy Cohort data reported major congenital malformations in 2.8% (95% CI: 2.1%–3.7%) of 2,132 first-trimester monotherapy exposures; late-pregnancy exposure carries a small risk of postpartum hemorrhage and transient neonatal adaptation syndrome, while pregnancy clearance increases modestly.
Duloxetine Postpartum: Monitor newborns exposed in the third trimester for transient neonatal adaptation signs (jitteriness, poor feeding, tachypnea) for 48 hours and return any pregnancy-escalated maternal dose to baseline postpartum.
Ibuprofen & Celecoxib Pregnancy Warning: Both ibuprofen and celecoxib must be avoided from 20 weeks gestation onward due to fetal renal dysfunction and oligohydramnios, and are strictly contraindicated at 30 weeks and later because of premature closure of the fetal ductus arteriosus.
Tramadol Registry & Peripartum Risk: The Swedish Medical Birth Register reported major congenital malformations in 3.5% (95% CI: 2.6%–4.5%) of 1,751 first-trimester exposures; chronic or peripartum tramadol use should be avoided due to neonatal opioid withdrawal syndrome and neonatal respiratory depression.
Tramadol Postpartum: Avoid prolonged postpartum tramadol use while breastfeeding, especially in CYP2D6 ultrarapid metabolizers, and taper maternal dose promptly after acute post-delivery pain resolves.
Metoprolol Preferred Beta-Blocker & Registry: Metoprolol is a preferred beta-blocker for migraine prophylaxis or maternal cardiovascular indications during pregnancy when therapy is required; Nordic and Medicaid Beta-Blocker Cohort data reported major congenital malformations in 3.2% (95% CI: 2.7%–3.8%) of 4,890 first-trimester exposures.
Metoprolol Pregnancy PK & Postpartum: CYP2D6-mediated metoprolol clearance increases markedly (2- to 4-fold) during the second and third trimesters, sometimes requiring higher divided doses; monitor third-trimester fetal growth and neonatal bradycardia/hypoglycemia, and reduce pregnancy-escalated doses promptly postpartum to prevent maternal bradycardia and hypotension.

Pyridostigmine Preferred MG Therapy & PK: Pyridostigmine is the preferred first-line symptomatic treatment for myasthenia gravis throughout pregnancy; expanded maternal blood volume, increased renal clearance, and delayed gastric emptying in the second and third trimesters frequently require dose escalation or shortened dosing intervals, while IV cholinesterase inhibitors near term should be avoided to prevent premature uterine contractions.
Pyridostigmine Postpartum: Maternal pyridostigmine requirements often fluctuate sharply in the immediate postpartum period; closely monitor myasthenic strength and adjust back toward pre-pregnancy oral dosing within days to weeks after delivery while watching newborns for transient neonatal myasthenia gravis.
Prednisolone & Methylprednisolone Registry & PK: Non-fluorinated corticosteroids (prednisolone and methylprednisolone) undergo ~88%–90% placental inactivation by 11-beta-hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase type 2 (11-beta-HSD2), making them preferred systemic corticosteroids in pregnancy; UKTIS and OTIS cohorts reported major congenital malformations in 3.4% (95% CI: 2.5%–4.5%) of 1,427 first-trimester systemic exposures.
Prednisolone & Methylprednisolone Postpartum: Screen for maternal gestational diabetes and hypertension during pregnancy; postpartum, taper high-dose relapse steroids cautiously (keeping in mind that postpartum rebound flares of myasthenia gravis and MS/NMOSD are common) while ensuring perioperative stress-dose coverage during labor if chronically suppressed.
Dexamethasone Placental Transfer & PK: Unlike prednisolone, dexamethasone is a fluorinated corticosteroid that is minimally metabolized by placental 11-beta-HSD2 and crosses freely to the fetus; reserve systemic maternal dexamethasone for acute CNS edema or specific fetal lung maturation indications, using the lowest effective duration.
Azathioprine Registry & PK: Azathioprine is an acceptable steroid-sparing maintenance immunosuppressant during pregnancy in severe myasthenia gravis or NMOSD because the fetal liver lacks inosinate pyrophosphorylase to form active 6-thioguanine nucleotides; Transplant and Autoimmune Pregnancy Registry data reported major congenital malformations in 3.6% (95% CI: 2.6%–4.9%) of 1,148 first-trimester exposures.
Azathioprine Pregnancy Monitoring & Postpartum: Monitor maternal CBC and liver transaminases regularly during pregnancy (6-TGN levels may decrease while 6-MMP increases slightly); continue stable maintenance dosing postpartum to prevent autoimmune relapse while checking neonatal blood counts if late-pregnancy maternal cytopenia occurred.
Mycophenolate Registries & PK: Mycophenolate mofetil is contraindicated in pregnancy due to severe embryopathy (microtia, cleft lip/palate, congenital heart and esophageal defects) and ~45%–49% miscarriage rate; National Transplant Pregnancy Registry (NTPR) data showed major malformations in 23.0% (95% CI: 16.0%–31.5%) of 130 first-trimester exposures, and the ENTIS cohort reported 26.7% (95% CI: 15.6%–40.3%) of 56 exposures.
Rituximab Registry, Placental Transfer & Postpartum: The Rituximab Global Drug Safety Pregnancy Database reported major congenital malformations in 2.6% (95% CI: 1.0%–5.6%) of 231 pre-conception or first-trimester exposures; because IgG1 crosses the placenta actively in the 2nd/3rd trimesters via FcRn, third-trimester exposure causes transient neonatal B-cell depletion—check neonatal B-cell counts at birth, delay live vaccines until B-cell recovery, and resume maternal dosing postpartum when clinically indicated to prevent NMOSD/MS rebound.
IVIG Preferred Immunomodulation & PK: Intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG) is a preferred immunomodulatory therapy for myasthenia gravis crisis, CIDP, and NMOSD relapses during pregnancy; endogenous IgG crosses the placenta via FcRn after 30–32 weeks without teratogenicity, and pregnancy management requires monitoring maternal hydration and venous thromboembolism prophylaxis.
Eculizumab Registry, PK & Postpartum: The International PNH and aHUS/NMOSD Pregnancy Registry reported major congenital malformations in 2.0% (95% CI: 0.4%–5.8%) of 148 pregnancy exposures; terminal complement activity and volume of distribution rise in the third trimester, occasionally requiring shortened dosing intervals or supplemental doses, followed by return to standard biweekly maintenance postpartum.
Ravulizumab Pregnancy Safety & PK: Ravulizumab is an engineered long-acting IgG2/4 monoclonal antibody that crosses the placenta via FcRn in late pregnancy; human registry data remain sparse compared with eculizumab, so reserve use during pregnancy for severe NMOSD or gMG where maternal benefit justifies fetal complement blockade and return to standard 8-weekly maintenance postpartum.
Efgartigimod Pregnancy Safety & PK: Efgartigimod blocks neonatal FcRn, reducing both maternal pathogenic IgG and transplacental transfer of protective maternal IgG antibodies to the fetus; human pregnancy data are lacking, so evaluate neonatal serum IgG levels and delay live infant vaccines if exposed in the third trimester.
Baclofen Pregnancy Safety & Postpartum: Oral baclofen has limited human teratogenicity data and crosses the placenta, whereas intrathecal baclofen minimizes systemic fetal exposure; abrupt cessation risks severe withdrawal, and newborns exposed chronically in late pregnancy must be monitored for neonatal baclofen withdrawal (hypertonia, irritability, seizures) during the first week postpartum.
Tizanidine Pregnancy Safety & PK: Human pregnancy data for tizanidine are insufficient to define malformation risk, while animal studies showed embryo-fetal toxicity and prolonged gestation at supratherapeutic doses; reserve only if maternal spasticity benefit outweighs risk, and monitor CYP1A2-mediated interaction with estrogen postpartum if combined oral contraceptives are resumed.
Dantrolene Pregnancy Safety & PK: Dantrolene crosses the placenta (fetal-to-maternal ratio ~0.4); while chronic oral pregnancy data are limited, emergency intravenous dantrolene is life-saving and must never be withheld in pregnant patients with malignant hyperthermia or neuroleptic malignant syndrome, watching the newborn briefly for transient hypotonia postpartum.
