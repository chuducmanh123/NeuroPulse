Pregnancy, Teratology, and Peripartum Pharmacology in Cerebrovascular, Neurocritical Care, Movement, Cognitive, and Neuro-Infectious Disorders: International Guidelines (AHA/ASA, ESC, EHRA, IDSA, ATA, ADA) and Pregnancy Cohorts

=== SECTION 1: PRECONCEPTION & CONTRACEPTIVE INTERACTIONS ===
No Hormonal Contraceptive Induction (Cardiovascular, Antithrombotic, Metabolic, PPI & Movement Agents): None of the standard oral anticoagulants, antiplatelets, antihypertensives, statins, antidiabetic agents, proton pump inhibitors, or dopaminergic medications reduce hormonal contraceptive efficacy via CYP3A4 or UGT induction.
Teratogenic Agents Requiring Effective Contraception (Warfarin, DOACs, ACEi/ARBs, Statins, Amiodarone, Spironolactone, Amantadine): Effective contraception is essential prior to conception when prescribing warfarin, direct oral anticoagulants, lisinopril, losartan, valsartan, statins, amiodarone, spironolactone, or amantadine so therapy can be switched to pregnancy-safe alternatives before pregnancy occurs.
Broad-Spectrum Antibiotics Contraception Note (Ampicillin, Amoxicillin-Clavulanate, Ceftriaxone, Piperacillin-Tazobactam, Azithromycin, Vancomycin, Ciprofloxacin, Levofloxacin): Unlike rifamycins, non-rifamycin beta-lactams, macrolides, glycopeptides, and fluoroquinolones do not reduce systemic ethinylestradiol or progestin levels; extra backup contraception is only required if severe antibiotic-associated vomiting or diarrhea impairs pill absorption.
Folic Acid Preconception Baseline (Warfarin, Metformin, Diabetes): Standard preconception folic acid supplementation (0.4 mg/day) applies generally, while higher preconception folate (up to 4–5 mg/day) may be considered in pre-existing diabetes mellitus due to elevated baseline neural tube defect risk; metformin does not require routine high-dose folate beyond B12 monitoring.

=== SECTION 2: ANTICOAGULANTS, ANTIPLATELETS, THROMBOLYTICS & REVERSAL ===
Warfarin Registry, PK & Postpartum: Warfarin crosses the placenta causing warfarin embryopathy (nasal hypoplasia, stippled epiphyses) in weeks 6–12 and fetal CNS hemorrhage later; ENTIS Prospective Cohort reported major congenital malformations in 7.4% (95% CI: 4.5%–11.8%, n=229) of first-trimester exposures; switch to LMWH during weeks 6–12 or throughout pregnancy, and warfarin may be safely resumed immediately postpartum as it does not pass into breast milk.
DOACs Pregnancy Contraindication (Apixaban, Dabigatran, Edoxaban, Rivaroxaban): Direct oral anticoagulants (apixaban, dabigatran, edoxaban, rivaroxaban) cross the human placenta, carry unacceptable fetal bleeding and embryotoxicity risk, and are contraindicated/avoided during pregnancy per ESC and ASH guidelines; switch immediately to LMWH upon pregnancy recognition, and transition back postpartum only after breastfeeding decisions are reviewed.
Low-Dose Aspirin Pregnancy & Registry: Low-dose aspirin (75–150 mg/day) is safe and recommended for preeclampsia prevention and maternal stroke/antiplatelet indications (`caution` for high analgesic doses >=300 mg/day near term due to ductal closure); Nordic and US Collaborative Cohorts reported major congenital malformations in 3.0% (95% CI: 2.6%–3.4%, n=6,102) of low-dose exposures.
Clopidogrel & Ticagrelor Pregnancy & Peripartum: Clopidogrel (and ticagrelor) have limited human pregnancy data; clopidogrel is reserved for essential coronary or carotid/intracranial stent indications (`caution`) and must be held 5 to 7 days before neuraxial anesthesia or delivery to prevent maternal/neonatal hemorrhage, then resumed postpartum once hemostasis is secure.
Thrombolytics (Alteplase & Tenecteplase): Alteplase (~59 kDa) and tenecteplase (~59 kDa) are large recombinant proteins that do not cross the human placenta; per AHA/ASA Acute Stroke Guidelines, IV thrombolysis may be considered in pregnant patients with disabling acute ischemic stroke when anticipated benefit outweighs maternal-fetal hemorrhage risk (`caution`), with acute postpartum bleeding vigilance if given peripartum.
Reversal Agents (Phytonadione, Protamine, 4F-PCC, Idarucizumab): Phytonadione (vitamin K1) is preferred and essential to reverse maternal/neonatal coagulopathy (`preferred`); protamine sulfate reverses UFH/LMWH without placental transfer; 4F-PCC and idarucizumab (~48 kDa) are life-saving emergency reversal agents for refractory maternal hemorrhage and should never be withheld in pregnancy (`caution`).

=== SECTION 3: ANTIHYPERTENSIVES, ANTIARRHYTHMICS, DIURETICS & ELECTROLYTES ===
Labetalol Preferred First-Line & Registry: Labetalol is a preferred first-line oral and IV antihypertensive for chronic hypertension and preeclampsia in pregnancy per ACOG and ESC guidelines; Nordic and Medicaid Birth Cohorts reported major congenital malformations in 2.9% (95% CI: 2.4%–3.5%, n=3,842, first-trimester exposure); monitor newborns for transient hypoglycemia/bradycardia and adjust postpartum blood pressure therapy over days 3–6 when postpartum hypertension peaks.
Calcium Channel Blockers (Amlodipine, Nicardipine, Nimodipine): Extended-release nifedipine and amlodipine are acceptable options for gestational hypertension (`caution`; Nordic/Medicaid cohort for amlodipine/CCBs: 3.1% MCM, 95% CI: 2.1%–4.5%, n=912); IV nicardipine is used for refractory hypertensive emergencies; nimodipine is reserved for aneurysmal SAH vasospasm during pregnancy (`caution`), monitoring maternal blood pressure and uteroplacental perfusion, with postpartum titration as BP stabilizes.
Beta-Blockers (Bisoprolol & Nebivolol): Bisoprolol and nebivolol have smaller human pregnancy datasets than labetalol or metoprolol (`caution`); maternal CYP2D6/renal clearance changes may alter exposure, third-trimester use warrants fetal growth and neonatal bradycardia/hypoglycemia monitoring, and pregnancy-escalated doses should be reduced postpartum.
Digoxin Pregnancy PK & Postpartum: Digoxin crosses the placenta without teratogenicity (`caution`); renal clearance and CYP/P-gp activity increase 20%–50% during pregnancy (lowering serum digoxin levels), requiring therapeutic drug monitoring and occasional dose escalation, followed by postpartum dose reduction over 1 to 2 weeks to avoid digoxin toxicity.
Amiodarone Pregnancy Contraindication: Amiodarone crosses the placenta with high iodine content and prolonged half-life (~40–55 days), causing fetal/neonatal hypothyroidism (up to 9%–20%), goiter, bradycardia, and neurodevelopmental delay (`avoid`); reserve strictly for life-threatening maternal refractory arrhythmias and check neonatal thyroid function postpartum.
ACEi & ARBs Fetotoxicity (Lisinopril, Losartan, Valsartan): Lisinopril, losartan, and valsartan are strictly contraindicated in pregnancy (`avoid`), especially during the 2nd and 3rd trimesters when RAAS blockade causes fetal renal tubular dysgenesis, oligohydramnios, pulmonary hypoplasia, skull hypoplasia, and fetal death; National Registry cohorts reported 5.6% (95% CI: 4.1%–7.6%, n=745) adverse fetal/malformation outcomes with first-trimester exposure; switch to labetalol or nifedipine preconception and monitor postpartum BP before any restart.
Spironolactone & Furosemide: Spironolactone exhibits anti-androgenic activity in animal models and must be avoided in pregnancy (`avoid`); furosemide is reserved for maternal pulmonary edema or heart failure (`caution`) because diuretic-induced intravascular volume depletion can impair uteroplacental perfusion, requiring postpartum volume reassessment.
Potassium Chloride: Oral and IV potassium chloride is physiological replacement therapy (`preferred`) safe throughout all trimesters when indicated for hypokalemia; monitor maternal serum potassium and renal function.

=== SECTION 4: STATINS, ENDOCRINE/METABOLIC & GASTROINTESTINAL ===
Statins (Atorvastatin, Rosuvastatin, Simvastatin): Although recent FDA labeling removed the absolute pregnancy contraindication box for extreme high-risk familial hypercholesterolemia or ASCVD, statins remain avoided in routine pregnancy (`avoid`) due to animal developmental toxicity and theoretical disruption of fetal cholesterol synthesis; discontinue when pregnancy is recognized and resume only after breastfeeding or per cardiology guidance.
Levothyroxine Preferred & Pregnancy PK/Postpartum: Levothyroxine is preferred and essential in pregnancy (`preferred`); maternal thyroid hormone demand rises 25%–50% by weeks 4–8 of gestation due to TBG elevation and placental deiodinase activity, requiring immediate 20%–30% dose increase upon positive pregnancy test, TSH monitoring every 4 weeks, and prompt reduction back to pre-pregnancy dose immediately postpartum.
Insulin Glargine Preferred & Postpartum: Insulin is the preferred gold-standard therapy for diabetes in pregnancy (`preferred`) because it does not cross the placenta; insulin requirements rise steeply in the 2nd and 3rd trimesters due to placental lactogen resistance, then drop precipitously by 30%–50% immediately after placental delivery—reduce postpartum basal insulin promptly to avoid severe maternal hypoglycemia.
Metformin Registry, PK & Postpartum: Metformin crosses the placenta freely without teratogenicity (`caution`); Nordic & UK Teratology Cohorts reported major congenital malformations in 3.2% (95% CI: 2.5%–4.1%, n=1,840, first-trimester exposure); renal clearance rises in pregnancy, and postpartum insulin sensitivity returns rapidly so discontinue or halve gestational doses after delivery while monitoring glycemia.
Sulfonylureas & Newer Antidiabetics (Gliclazide, Glimepiride, Sitagliptin, Dapagliflozin, Empagliflozin): Gliclazide and glimepiride cross the placenta and risk severe prolonged neonatal hypoglycemia (`avoid` for gliclazide, `caution` for glimepiride); sitagliptin has limited human data; dapagliflozin and empagliflozin must be avoided in 2nd/3rd trimesters (`avoid`) due to renal tubular developmental toxicity in animal kidneys—transition to insulin during pregnancy and reassess postpartum.
Proton Pump Inhibitors (Omeprazole, Esomeprazole, Pantoprazole): PPIs are widely used for severe GERD or stress ulcer prophylaxis in pregnancy (`caution`); Danish National Birth Cohort data for omeprazole reported major congenital malformations in 2.6% (95% CI: 2.2%–3.1%, n=1,800) and pantoprazole 2.9% (95% CI: 1.9%–4.3%, n=549) with no elevated birth defect risk.

=== SECTION 5: CNS INFECTIONS — ANTIMICROBIALS ===
Penicillins & Cephalosporins (Ampicillin, Amoxicillin-Clavulanate, Ceftriaxone, Piperacillin-Tazobactam): Beta-lactams are preferred first-line antibiotics in pregnancy (`preferred` for ampicillin, amoxicillin-clavulanate, ceftriaxone; `caution` for piperacillin-tazobactam due to smaller human cohorts); Danish/OTIS Cohorts reported 2.7% MCM (95% CI: 2.4%–3.1%, n=12,500) for penicillins and 2.8% (95% CI: 2.1%–3.7%, n=1,740) for cephalosporins; increased maternal GFR and volume of distribution in pregnancy may warrant standard-to-high end dosing for CNS meningitis.
Azithromycin Preferred Macrolide: Azithromycin is a preferred macrolide in pregnancy (`preferred`) with lower risk than clarithromycin; OTIS and Nordic Pregnancy Cohorts reported major congenital malformations in 3.0% (95% CI: 2.5%–3.6%, n=4,200, first-trimester exposure).
Vancomycin Pregnancy PK & TDM: Vancomycin crosses the placenta without recognized teratogenicity (`caution`) and is essential for MRSA CNS infections; elevated maternal volume of distribution and augmented renal clearance in pregnancy increase vancomycin clearance, requiring AUC-guided or trough therapeutic drug monitoring and postpartum reassessment if therapy continues.
Fluoroquinolones (Ciprofloxacin & Levofloxacin): Fluoroquinolones are avoided as first-line agents in pregnancy (`caution`) due to arthropathy in juvenile animal cartilage, though ENTIS Prospective Cohort data (2.8% MCM, 95% CI: 1.9%–4.1%, n=949) showed no clear human teratogenic increase; reserve for severe resistant CNS or life-threatening infections when safer beta-lactams cannot be used.

=== SECTION 6: MOVEMENT DISORDERS & DEMENTIA ===
Levodopa-Carbidopa Pregnancy & Registry: Levodopa-carbidopa is the preferred symptomatic therapy when Parkinson's disease or dopa-responsive dystonia requires treatment in pregnancy (`caution` due to limited sample sizes); International Movement Disorders Pregnancy Series reported no clear pattern of human malformations above baseline (~3% across small cohorts), and delayed gastric emptying in late pregnancy may require dose spacing.
Dopamine Agonists & Adjuncts (Pramipexole, Ropinirole, Rasagiline, Entacapone, Trihexyphenidyl): Pramipexole, ropinirole, rasagiline, entacapone, and trihexyphenidyl have insufficient human pregnancy safety data (`caution`); dopamine agonists suppress maternal prolactin and inhibit postpartum lactation, so transition to levodopa monotherapy if breastfeeding is planned.
Amantadine Teratogenicity: Amantadine is teratogenic in animals and has case reports of complex congenital heart defects (including single ventricle and truncus arteriosus) in humans (`avoid`); avoid during pregnancy and switch to safer alternatives before conception.
Dementia Therapies (Donepezil, Rivastigmine, Memantine, Lecanemab, Donanemab): Acetylcholinesterase inhibitors (donepezil, rivastigmine), memantine, and anti-amyloid IgG1 monoclonal antibodies (lecanemab, donanemab) lack adequate human pregnancy data (`caution` — largely indicated in older postmenopausal populations); anti-amyloid IgG1 antibodies cross the placenta in late pregnancy via FcRn and carry prolonged washout considerations if ever used in reproductive-age carriers.
