American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG) Practice Bulletin No. 196: Thromboembolism in Pregnancy. Obstet Gynecol. 2018;132(1):e1–e17.

=== WARFARIN: PRECONCEPTION, GESTATIONAL SWITCHING & DELIVERY PREPARATION (p. e4, e10) ===
Warfarin First-Trimester Embryopathy & LMWH Switch (p. e4): Warfarin embryopathy has been linked with exposure at 6–12 weeks of gestation, highlighting the importance of pre-pregnancy and early pregnancy care in patients using warfarin. Therefore, for most women receiving prolonged anticoagulation therapy who become pregnant, it is recommended that low-molecular-weight heparin be used in place of warfarin.
Warfarin Mechanical Heart Valve Regimens (p. e4): Regimens include adjusted-dose low-molecular-weight heparin or unfractionated heparin throughout pregnancy or from the 6th week until the 13th week with substitution by vitamin K antagonists until close to delivery when low-molecular-weight heparin or unfractionated heparin is resumed.
Warfarin Unexpected Delivery & Neonatal Reversal (p. e4): The risk of fetal hemorrhage in women on warfarin appears to be greatest around the time of delivery; therefore, if delivery unexpectedly occurs while a woman is receiving a vitamin K antagonist, cesarean delivery may be required and the neonate may require administration of vitamin K and fresh frozen plasma.
Warfarin Postpartum Bridging & Dosing (p. e10): Women who will be treated with warfarin should be bridged with adjusted-dose low-molecular-weight heparin or unfractionated heparin until an international normalized ratio in the therapeutic range (2.0–3.0) is achieved for 2 consecutive days; the initial dose of warfarin is 5 mg daily for 2 days, with subsequent doses determined by monitoring the international normalized ratio.
Warfarin Postpartum Resumption Timing (p. e10): For women with mechanical heart valves, warfarin can be resumed 24 hours after delivery, with overlapping intravenous unfractionated heparin (or low-molecular-weight heparin) until therapeutic on warfarin; patients without mechanical heart valves can be switched when postpartum bleeding risk has subsided (usually 1–2 weeks).
Warfarin Lactation Safety (p. e10–e11): Because warfarin, low-molecular-weight heparin, and unfractionated heparin do not accumulate in breast milk and do not induce an anticoagulant effect in the infant, these anticoagulants are compatible with breastfeeding.

=== DOACs (DABIGATRAN, RIVAROXABAN, APIXABAN, EDOXABAN): PREGNANCY & POSTPARTUM (p. e4, e10) ===
DOACs Placental & Milk Transfer (p. e4): Oral direct thrombin inhibitors (dabigatran) and anti-Xa inhibitors (rivaroxaban, apixaban, edoxaban, betrixaban) should be avoided in pregnancy and lactation because there are insufficient data to evaluate safety for the woman, fetus, and breastfeeding neonate; ex vivo studies demonstrate transfer across the placenta and detectable levels in human milk.
DOACs Prepregnancy / Early Switch to LMWH (p. e4): Until more data are available, women should be transitioned to low-molecular-weight heparin prepregnancy or as soon as possible during pregnancy.
DOACs Postpartum Non-Breastfeeding Option (p. e10): Women who require more than 6 weeks of postpartum anticoagulation therapy may be bridged to warfarin or a direct oral anticoagulant if not breastfeeding.

=== DELIVERY & NEURAXIAL ANESTHESIA BRIDGE TIMING FOR HEPARIN / LMWH (p. e8–e10, Table 4) ===
LMWH / UFH Pre-Delivery & Conversion Timing (p. e8, e11): For women who are receiving prophylactic low-molecular-weight heparin, discontinuation is recommended at least 12 hours before scheduled induction of labor or cesarean delivery; a 24-hour interval is recommended for patients on an adjusted-dose regimen, or women may be converted from low-molecular-weight heparin to unfractionated heparin in anticipation of delivery.
Postpartum Anticoagulation Restart Timing (p. e10–e11): A reasonable approach to minimize postpartum bleeding complications is resumption of anticoagulation therapy no sooner than 4–6 hours after vaginal delivery or 6–12 hours after cesarean delivery (and at least 24 hours after neuraxial blockade and 4 hours after catheter removal for therapeutic LMWH).

=== PROTAMINE SULFATE & PHYTONADIONE REVERSAL IN PREGNANCY (p. e4, e9) ===
Protamine Sulfate Reversal (p. e9): Protamine sulfate can be used to reverse unfractionated heparin or, less predictably, low-molecular-weight heparin; reversal of heparin, however, is rarely required and is not indicated with a prophylactic dose of heparin.
