=== TABLE 2: ANTIHYPERTENSIVE MEDICATIONS IN PREGNANT & POSTPARTUM INDIVIDUALS (p. e5–e6) ===
Hypertension Goal in Pregnancy: Evidence supports treating chronic hypertension in pregnancy before it reaches severe levels, targeting a goal blood pressure <140/90 mmHg.
Labetalol in Pregnancy (Table 2): First-line agent Labetalol: starting dose 200 mg 2–3 times a day, maximum daily dose 2400 mg/d, titration interval every 2–3 d.
Labetalol Postpartum & Lactation (Table 2): Postpartum first-line agent Labetalol: starting dose 200 mg 2–3 times a day, maximum dose 2400 mg/d, titration every 2–3 d; Lactation safety: Safe RID 3.6%.
Amlodipine Postpartum & Lactation (Table 2): Postpartum first-line agent Amlodipine: starting dose 5 mg daily, maximum dose 10 mg/d, titration every 5–7 d; Lactation safety: Safe RID 1.7%–4.3%.
Furosemide Postpartum & Lactation (Table 2): Postpartum alternative agent Furosemide: starting dose 10 mg daily, maximum dose 160 mg/d (2- or 3-times-a-day dosing), titration every 3–5 d; Lactation safety: Safe RID; may decrease breastmilk production (dose >20 mg/d).
Spironolactone Postpartum & Lactation (Table 2): Postpartum alternative agent Spironolactone: starting dose 12.5 mg daily, titration every 14–30 d; Lactation safety: Safe RID 2%–4.3%.
Metoprolol Tartrate Postpartum & Lactation (Table 2): Postpartum alternative agent Metoprolol tartrate: starting dose 12.5 mg twice daily, maximum dose 200 mg twice daily, titration every 2–3 d; Lactation safety: Limited safety data, likely low risk.
Bisoprolol Postpartum & Lactation (Table 2): Postpartum alternative agent Bisoprolol: starting dose 2.5 mg daily, maximum dose 20 mg/d, titration every 5–7 d; Lactation safety: Limited safety data.
RAAS & MRA Pregnancy Contraindication (Table 2): Angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors, angiotensin receptor blockers, direct renin inhibitors, and mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists are contraindicated in pregnancy because of the potential for fetal harm.
Lisinopril, Losartan, Valsartan Postpartum (Table 2): Postpartum alternative agents Lisinopril (2.5–40 mg/d), Losartan (25–100 mg/d), and Valsartan (20–320 mg/d), titrated every 5–7 d, have limited safety data during lactation.

=== TABLE 2: ANTITHROMBOTIC AGENTS & STATINS IN PREGNANCY AND LACTATION (p. e5–e9) ===
Aspirin Preeclampsia & Secondary Stroke Prevention (p. e5, e8, e9): Daily low-dose aspirin has been shown in meta-analysis to significantly reduce the risk of preeclampsia in high-risk individuals compared with placebo (pooled risk ratio 0.85 [95% CI, 0.75–0.95]); low-dose aspirin is considered safe for most pregnant patients with prior ischemic stroke or TIA.
Aspirin Pregnancy & Lactation (Table 2): Aspirin <100 mg daily: Safe; first choice antiplatelet agent. No need to discontinue before regional anesthesia. Lactation safety: May be used for cardiovascular indications; subclinical amounts in breastmilk.
Clopidogrel Pregnancy & Lactation (Table 2): Clopidogrel 75 mg daily: Use for shortest duration necessary. Animal studies show no adverse effects; limited human data. Stop 5–7 d before regional anesthesia; may be resumed immediately after (no loading dose) unless high bleeding risk. Lactation safety: Assess risk/benefit; low risk of infant harm based on limited human data and drug properties.
Ticagrelor Pregnancy & Lactation (Table 2): Ticagrelor 90 mg twice daily: Minimal data; ticagrelor does cross placenta. Stop 5 d before regional anesthesia; neuraxial catheters should not be maintained with ticagrelor because of the rapid onset; may be resumed immediately after (no loading dose) unless high bleeding risk.
Ticagrelor Lactation (Table 2): No published information is available on the use of ticagrelor during breastfeeding. Because ticagrelor and its active metabolite are >99% bound to plasma proteins, the amount in milk is likely to be low.
Warfarin Pregnancy & Neuraxial Anesthesia (Table 2): Warfarin <5 mg in pregnancy; variable in lactation: Teratogenic in first trimester; may be considered after first trimester for select patients (mechanical valve). Stop 5 d before planned procedures and ensure INR is in normal range before neuraxial anesthesia; remove neuraxial catheters when INR <1.5. Lactation safety: Safe; unlikely excreted into breastmilk.
DOACs Pregnancy & Lactation (Table 2): Dabigatran, Rivaroxaban, Apixaban: Insufficient data in pregnancy and insufficient data in lactation.
Statins Pregnancy & Lactation (Table 2): Statins (atorvastatin, rosuvastatin, simvastatin): Pregnancy safety: Assess risk/benefit; no evidence of teratogenicity or increased major birth defects. Lactation safety: Not recommended due to concern about disruption of infant lipid metabolism.

=== THROMBOLYSIS, CONTRACEPTION & POSTPARTUM MANAGEMENT (p. e10, e12, e14) ===
Alteplase & Tenecteplase in Pregnancy (p. e10): US-based guidelines support the use of intravenous alteplase during pregnancy when the potential benefits (eg, prevention of long-term disability) outweigh the risks (eg, uterine bleeding). Alteplase and tenecteplase do not cross the placenta, and case reports suggest good fetal outcomes after treatment; however, data on tenecteplase use in pregnancy remain limited.
Thrombolysis Early Postpartum (p. e10): Decisions during the early postpartum period should be individualized, especially in patients with recent cesarean delivery or neuraxial anesthesia due to bleeding risk.
Postpartum Vasospasm Avoidance (p. e12): Postpartum patients with stroke may be at higher risk for cerebral vasospasm, especially those with preeclampsia, RCVS, recent SAH, or recent mechanical thrombectomy; avoid sympathomimetics, serotonergic medications, and ergot derivatives (eg, methylergonovine) if possible.
Post-Stroke Contraception Guidance (p. e12, e14): Estrogen-containing contraceptives (pill, patch, ring) are associated with an increased stroke risk, whereas progestin-only methods (oral, IUD, implant) and nonhormonal options (copper IUD, barrier methods) have not been shown to significantly increase arterial or venous thrombosis or stroke risk.
