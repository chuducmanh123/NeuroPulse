Title: Chronic Anticholinergic Use and the Aging Brain
Authors: Noll L. Campbell, Malaz A. Boustani, Kathleen A. Lane, Sujuan Gao, Hugh C. Hendrie, Babar A. Khan, Jill R. Murrell, Frederick W. Unverzagt, Anthony M. Hake, Valerie Smith-Gamble, Kathleen S. Hall
Journal: Alzheimer's & Dementia 9 (2013) 377-385
DOI: 10.1016/j.jalz.2012.02.005

Abstract and Findings:
The Anticholinergic Cognitive Burden (ACB) scale quantifies anticholinergic adverse exposure in older adults. A definite anticholinergic cognitive burden is defined by an ACB score of 3 or greater. Exposure to medications with possible or definite anticholinergic activity with an cumulative ACB score of 3 or greater is associated with increased risk of cognitive impairment, incident dementia, and mortality.
